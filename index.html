Job Aid - Loading and Unloading

This job aid outlines the loading and unloading process for the 2-Car Hauler, supporting your safety and that of others on the road.

# **Use this Job Aid as a guide on how to load and unload the 2-car hauler.**

Non-Negotiable Safety Rules for Loading and Unloading

To support our Associates' safety, we have 15 rules to follow each time a Driver loads or unloads a vehicle. These requirements are intended to help protect our Associates, Vehicles, and Trucks.

Additional Safety Items when using the 2 Car

# Deck:

•
Use Caution with Twisting Motions: Avoid twisting while climbing, descending, entering or exiting vehicles, and securing vehicles to prevent strain or loss of balance.
•
Watch Your Footing: Always use the ladder system to ascend or descend from the cab or deck. Never walk on the deck or jump off.
•
Use Designated Access Points: Only use ladders and areas with non-skid surfaces when moving on or off the deck.
•
Maintain Three Points of Contact: Use grab handles and ensure two hands and one foot, or two feet and one hand are in contact at all times for stability.
•
Avoid Unsafe Areas when walking on the deck.
•
Weather Advisory: Use extra caution in wet, oily, icy, or windy conditions. Slow down and adjust movements accordingly.
•
Winter Safety Measures: Use salt or de-icing spray to treat icy surfaces. Wash off salt promptly to prevent corrosion.

# Tools:

•
Inspect the ratcheting system to ensure there are no obstructions and that it is in proper operating condition.
•
Tie-down straps must be properly rated for the weight of the vehicle being secured.
•
Strap condition: Replace damaged or worn straps immediately. Check for signs of wear and tear such as burn marks, fraying, cuts or tears, mold or mildew, and broken hooks. Ensure the load limit tag is still attached for inspection and compliance.

# Equipment:

•
Do not exceed the factory pressure setting on the hydraulic system as indicated on the warning label on your equipment.

# Driving on the Deck

Driving on the bed of an auto hauler requires slow, intentional movement, precise vehicle placement, and complete focus to prevent accidents and ensure safe transport.

•
Distractions - Remove all distractions while operating on or around the truck and trailer (i.e., no cellphones or headsets).
•
Adverse Conditions - Use extra caution when wet, oily, or icy conditions are present.
•
Vehicle Safety - Prior to loading a vehicle onto a trailer, verify it is in a safe operating condition and has fully functional brakes to prevent accidents.
•
Secure floor mats - Remove floor mats or loose items that could interfere with the accelerator.
•
Center the Vehicle - Use deck holes to align the vehicle and step out to verify it is centered.
•
Operate at a Controlled Speed - Maintain a slow and steady pace while keeping the vehicle centered on the trailer.
•
Best Practice Procedures:
Load vehicles using four-wheel drive if the vehicle is equipped.
Engage the parking brake before exiting.
Secure vehicle (all windows up) and doors are locked.
Store all keys in your pockets before exiting the loading vehicle.
Keep your hands free of objects to allow for maximum grip.
Prior to moving a vehicle, HONK to make others aware, especially if you are reversing.
•
Monitor Vehicle Placement - When loading vehicles onto the 'trailer/bed' carefully monitor their position to prevent damage to mirrors, doors, tires, and vehicle sides. If necessary, fold mirrors inward to allow for safe clearance.
•
Ideal Strap Placement: Keep all straps positioned to the sides of the deck plates. Do not drive over wheel straps or hooks.
•
Manual transmission vehicles - Engage the parking brake, and set the vehicle to 1st or reverse gear, depending on the incline.
•
Side Rails - Vehicles should be centered on the truck/within the parameters of the securement area for safe operations. Drivers must use ladders and grab bars to safely egress and regress.
•
Narrow Vehicles - Keep wheels centered per hole guides on deck when loading narrow vehicles (e.g. Smartcar) to prevent slipping or falling through the gap.
•
Trailer Support Beams and Clearance - Always watch for trailer support beams, roof clearances, and hinge points.
★
Prioritize safety by watching your surroundings, inspecting the unit prior to loading, driving slowly, removing distractions, and being cautious in poor conditions.

1.
Retrieve Materials - Gather BOL envelopes (with titles, if applicable) and keys for all vehicles.
Verify stock and VIN numbers of all vehicles being moved.
Inspect each vehicle for damage PRIOR to loading.
2.

3.
Load Placement - Plan the placement of vehicles on the trailer. Below is a typical arrangement.
4.
Prevent Cosmetic Damage - Be mindful of any zippers, pens, and other equipment on your body that could scratch a vehicle while loading and unloading.
5.
Walkaround vehicles - Before loading each vehicle, perform a walkaround to verify:
The vehicle operates correctly without issues affecting proper loading, like functional brakes, no flat or low tires, and secured convertible tops.

All parts, including hubcaps, center caps, mirrors, and antennas, are present.
6.
Partner with Functional Experts or refer to the loading guide if unsure how to arrange your load.

# Plan Load Placement

Planning how you will load the vehicles is a critical component of loading a trailer.

Steps:

1.
Identify all the vehicles to be loaded.
2.
Arrange the load considering these three factors:
Weight: Estimate how much each vehicle weighs. You can also refer to the Driver App for the estimated weight. Passenger cars tend to range between 3,200 and 3,600 lbs, while SUVs and trucks can easily exceed 4,500 lb. Electric Vehicles weigh more than gas/hybrid vehicles.
Size: Review the height and length of each vehicle. Ensure your load does not exceed the maximum allowable height for your location. Height allowance: 13' 6", though some states are 14'. Height may be shorter in some markets. Always review your route and note if you will need to decrease the height of your load based on the road conditions.
Route Plan: Understand what vehicles will be unloaded first and plan your load placement in the most efficient order. If possible, you only want to move the vehicle twice - once during loading and once during unloading.
3.
Below is a typical arrangement:
If moving two vehicles:
• First Vehicle: Small to midsize sedan or SUV, reversed in.
• Second Vehicle: Midsize to large sedan or SUV.
If moving one vehicle:
• Vehicle: Pull in forward as far as possible to balance axle weights.
*
For vehicles that don't start, contact management to follow the No Runner Standard Work.

# Loading Steps - Preparing the Truck

1.
Engage the PTO Switch - Engaging the PTO switch is done with the truck engine at low idle with the parking brakes applied.
If you need to run the cruise control, you can high idle to 1200 rpm by pressing the accel button.
2.
Secure the Cab - After exiting the cab, if you have two keys, lock the cab while performing the loading process.
3.
Personal Safety - Ensure only one person (primary operator) is on the trailer or operating its equipment (hydraulics) at all times. Any other parties must be on the ground away from the trailer and visible to the operator.
Only one person can be operating and loading/unloading the vehicle at one time. Additional parties can get vehicles prepped for loading. But only one person can be responsible for the entire loading/unloading process.
4.
Strap Placement - Move all straps away from the driving path to prevent damage and parking on top of them.
5.
Prepare the decks - Use your #1 lever to extend the rear ramp all the way out. Walk to the back and flip the flippers/skids onto the ground to load.

# Loading Steps

All vehicles are to be loaded with each deck at its lowest point. This will allow the driver to secure the vehicles from the ground and reduce the risk of injury. Ensure all decks are pinned correctly, even when not in use.

1.
Load - Reverse the vehicle onto the trailer into the #1 position, stop and check the center tracking, then continue reversing into position #1.
2.
Approach the #1 Position - Slowly back in until it makes contact with the tire stoppers, positioned on the 8th hole from the cab.
Ensure there is enough room between the car and the cab.
3.
Set the Vehicle - Prior to releasing the foot brake, place the vehicle in park and engage the parking brake.
Make sure all windows are rolled up.
Turn off the vehicle and lock the doors.
Secure the keys in your pocket until they can be stored in the cab.
4.
Secure the Driver's Side from the ground - Start with the front, then continue to the rear, and place a strap around each wheel.
5.
Secure the Passenger Side from the ground - Start with the rear, then continue to the front, and place a strap around each wheel.
6.
CarMax requires four tie-downs per vehicle.
Do not use the lasso strap/wheel loop strap system or the axle strap system to secure the vehicle.
Place the strap over the wheel. Ensure wheel straps are lying flat (not twisted) and running in a straight line from the ratchet shaft to the tire.
Set the strap hook as close to the tire as possible to act as a wheel chock and keep movement to a minimum. The open end of the strap hook should face away from the tire. Wheel straps must be tightened in opposing directions.
Ensure tabs of wheel strap cleats are in the center ribs of the tire tread.
Note on leaks: If there is a slow leak/loss of air pressure on a tire, you should not secure that tire with a strap. Three straps are acceptable and still meet DOT requirements. If the tire is completely flat, the vehicle should not be loaded (no-runner) unless a spare is installed.
*
Use the guide holes on each deck or ramp plate to ensure the vehicle is centered. Confirm that there is an equal number of holes visible on both sides of the vehicle to maintain proper alignment.

# Loading Steps (Continued)

7.
Tighten the straps - Take a two-point stance for stability, pull on the spool/ratchet handle to tighten the binder.
The binder bar should have at least one and a half wraps of strap on the binder spool after tightening for proper securement.
Do not twist the wheel strap during the securement process.
Do not use excessive force or over-tighten wheel straps (if a tire begins to bulge, you are overtightening—stop and restart).

Load the Second Vehicle:

1.
Set the #2 Deck - Walk to the hydraulic levers, use the #2 hydraulic lever, lift the #1 deck completely to ensure you have the clearance to load the Second Vehicle.
When it is completely raised, place the locking pins on both hydraulic arms and release pressure so the hydraulics rest on the safety pins.
2.
Load - Pull in forward into the #2 position, ensuring it is centered on the deck. Maintain the center by ensuring equal guide holes on both sides of the vehicle.
3.
Approach the #2 Position - Slowly pull in until the front tire lands in the solid square between the single flip-up anchor point and the multiple flip-up anchor points when properly parked.
4.
Set the Vehicle - Prior to releasing the foot brake, place the vehicle in park and engage the parking brake. Roll up all windows, turn off the vehicle, and secure keys in your pocket.
5.
Secure the Wheels from the ground - Exit and strap the driver and passenger wheels.

# Preparing the Decks & Final Prep

1.
Set the Flippers/Skids - Walk to the back and flip the flippers/skids both in.
2.
Slide in Rear Ramp - Use your #1 lever to retract the rear ramp all the way in.
3.
Lift the #2 deck - Use the #2 hydraulic lever to take the weight off the safety pins for repositioning.
4.
Set Pins - Once both pins are set, bring down the #1 deck slowly until it is resting on the safety pins. Ensure 6" clearance from the bottom of the vehicle to the top deck.

Prep for Drive:

1.
Measure Height - Using the height stick, measure the highest point of the load on the trailer (generally 13'6", 14' in some states). Know local height restrictions.
2.
Final Walkaround - Perform a walkaround, ensuring: all pins are set, all straps are tight, all equipment is put away, and there is clearance around each loaded vehicle.
3.
Switch Off the PTO.

Re-verify straps/chains are secure:

Drivers must re-verify that the straps/chains are properly tightened and vehicles secured at whichever of the following comes first:

1.
Every change of duty status.
2.
Every 3 hours of driving.
3.
Every 150 miles that are driven.

# Unloading Steps

Unstrap only the vehicles that need to be moved at the stop. Only work on one piece of equipment when loading and unloading.

Unload Second Vehicle:

1.
Switch On the PTO.
2.
Inspect - Walk down the passenger side and check for any road debris.
3.
Extend the rear ramps - Use the hydraulic levers to slide the #1 lever out to extend the rear ramps completely.
4.
Unfold the extension ramps.
5.
Raise the #2 deck - Use the #2 lever to raise the #2 deck completely. Lock both pins on the #2 deck.
6.
Unstrap - From the ground, unstrap the passenger's side wheels. Walk around the rear and unstrap the driver's side wheels on the second vehicle.
7.
Unload - Climb the ladder (maintaining 3 points of contact), get in, and slowly reverse the vehicle off the deck. Keep it centered. Do not solely rely on the backup camera. Ensure windows are up before exiting.
8.
Secure front wheel straps for the second vehicle beneath the #1 deck while the pins remain in place.
9.
Release the Pins - Return to the levers and raise the #1 deck to release the pins.

Unload the First Vehicle:

1.
Lower the #1 Deck - Release both pins and slowly lower the #1 deck, keeping clear of pinch points.
2.
Unstrap the First Vehicle - Unstrap both wheels on the passenger side, then both wheels on the driver side.
3.
Unload - Climb the ladder, get in, and slowly drive it off the deck. Ensure windows are up before exiting.
4.
Secure the Trailer - Flip up both extension ramps and secure straps on the passenger side. Slide in the #1 lever for the rear deck.

Secure the Trailer:

1.
Use your #1 lever to extend the rear ramp all the way out. Flip flippers/skids onto the ground.
2.
Flip the flippers/skids both in.
3.
Use the #2 lever to slide in the rear ramp. Lift the #2 deck using the #2 hydraulic lever to take the weight off the safety pins.
4.
Once both pins are set, bring down the #1 deck slowly until it rests on the safety pins.

# Transporting Electric Vehicles

Driving electric vehicles (EVs) on the bed of a hauler requires special attention to their unique features and safety considerations. The following instructions will guide you through the process, emphasizing the importance of gentle acceleration, seatbelt management, and using transport modes to minimize battery drain.

•
Be Mindful of One-Pedal Driving: Some EVs won't creep forward when your foot is off the brake, and the accelerator pedal is very responsive. Be mindful of one-pedal driving (uses only the accelerator to control both speed and braking) and gentle when applying the accelerator in an EV. Tesla vehicles have this feature enabled by default, and this feature can vary for other makes of EVs.
•
Unbuckle Seatbelts: Ensure EVs have all seatbelts unbuckled before transporting them. CarMax has found leaving a seatbelt buckled on an EV during transport can drain the battery, making it difficult to get the EV off the transporter at its destination.
•
Enable Transport or Shipping Modes: Some EVs (Rivian, Chevrolet EVs, etc.) have dedicated transport or shipping modes to minimize idle battery drain during transport. These can be enabled in the settings screen on the vehicle's infotainment screen, if available.
Manufacturer Guides: Rivian R1T & R1S Tow Operator Guide; Tesla: Instructions for Transporters.
Note: Tesla vehicles have a "Transport" mode, but it is only intended for use when winching a vehicle onto or off a hauler and not meant for use when the vehicle is actively being shipped.
★
Always prioritize being gentle with the accelerator, ensuring seatbelts are unbuckled, and using transport modes to minimize battery drain. If you have any questions or need further assistance, refer to the manufacturer's guides or seek additional training.

# Transporting Specialty Vehicles

Specialty vehicles are any vehicles that fall outside the normal size range, including modified vehicles or unmodified larger vehicles. The following instructions will guide you through the process of moving and handling specialty vehicles, emphasizing the importance of extra caution due to their unique clearances and step-in heights.

•
Extra Caution - Use extra caution when moving around and getting in and out of specialty vehicles, as the normal clearances and step-in heights can be drastically different from those of normal vehicles.
•
Loading Tall, Heavy, or Wide Vehicles - If moving especially tall, heavy, or wide vehicles (e.g., cargo vans, dually trucks), they will be the only vehicle loaded onto the truck. When moving a single vehicle, ensure it is fully pulled forward onto the wheel stopper behind the cab.
•
Locating Tires - Locate the front tires as you would a normal pick-up truck and ensure the inside rear wheels are located as normal single rear tires of a pick-up truck would be.
•
Vehicle Weights - Most vehicle weights can be found in the Black Book Invoicing Guides. These are available in the purchasing office or online at blackbookusa.com.
•
Verifying Height and Clearances - Always verify the height before transportation and know the clearances along your route. Read the clearance signs before crossing under any unfamiliar bridges, underpasses, or other restricted height obstructions.
•
Determining Newer Vehicle Weights - Newer vehicle weights can be determined by using the GVWR weight on the VIN tag and subtracting the cargo and occupant weight found on the **TIRE AND LOADING INFORMATION** tag located on the driver's door jamb.
★
Always prioritize using extra caution due to their unique clearances and step-in heights. Ensure that tall, heavy, or wide vehicles are loaded individually and fully pulled forward onto the wheel stopper. Verify vehicle weights and clearances to avoid any issues during transportation.
