Job Aid - Loading and Unloading - 2 Car Hauler

Operations & Safety Guidelines • Revised 11/7/25
Non-Negotiable Safety Rules: To support our Associates' safety, follow these 15 rules each time you load or unload. These protect Associates, Vehicles, and Trucks.

15 Safety Mandates

Deck Guidelines

1
Use Caution with Twisting Motions: Avoid twisting while climbing, descending, entering/exiting vehicles, and securing vehicles to prevent strain or loss of balance.
2
Watch Your Footing: Always use the ladder system to ascend or descend from the cab or deck. Never walk on the deck or jump off.
3
Use Designated Access Points: Only use ladders and areas with non-skid surfaces when moving on or off the deck.
4
Maintain Three Points of Contact: Use grab handles and ensure two hands and one foot, or two feet and one hand are in contact at all times for stability.
5
Avoid Unsafe Areas when walking on the deck.
6
Weather Advisory: Use extra caution in wet, oily, icy, or windy conditions. Slow down and adjust movements accordingly.
7
Winter Safety Measures: Use salt or de-icing spray to treat icy surfaces. Wash off salt promptly to prevent corrosion.

Tools & Equipment

8
Inspect Ratchets: Inspect the ratcheting system to ensure there are no obstructions and that it is in proper operating condition.
9
Weight Ratings: Tie-down straps must be properly rated for the weight of the vehicle being secured.
10
Strap Condition: Replace damaged or worn straps immediately. Check for:
Burn marks, cuts, or tears
Fraying, mold, or mildew
Broken hooks
Load limit tag (must be attached for inspection/compliance)
11
Hydraulic Pressure: Do not exceed the factory pressure setting on the hydraulic system as indicated on the warning label on your equipment.

Pre-Loading Steps

1
Retrieve Materials: Gather BOL envelopes (with titles, if applicable) and keys for all vehicles.
2
Verify STOCK & VIN: Verify stock and VIN numbers of all vehicles being moved.
3
Inspect Damage: Inspect each vehicle for damage PRIOR to loading.
4
Remove Lock Boxes: Ensure the lock box has been removed from the window of all vehicles to be transported and placed in the car.
5
Prevent Scratches: Be mindful of any zippers, pens, and other equipment on your body that could scratch a vehicle while loading and unloading.
6
Loading Guidance: Partner with Functional Experts or refer to the loading guide if unsure how to arrange your load.

Vehicle Walkaround

Before loading each vehicle, perform a walkaround to verify:

A
Functional Check: The vehicle operates correctly without issues affecting proper loading (e.g., functional brakes, no flat or low tires, and secured convertible tops).
B
Cosmetic Inspection: The vehicle is free of cosmetic damages such as dents, scrapes, loose body panels, cracked glass, or roof damage.
C
Present Parts: All parts, including hubcaps, center caps, mirrors, and antennas, are present.

Plan Load Placement

Planning how you will load the vehicles is a critical component of loading a trailer. Arrange the load considering these three factors:

1
Weight: Estimate how much each vehicle weighs. You can also refer to the Driver App for the estimated weight.
Passenger cars tend to range between 3,200 and 3,600 lbs.
SUVs and trucks can easily exceed 4,500 lbs.
Electric Vehicles (EVs) weigh more than gas/hybrid vehicles.
2
Size & Route Height: Review the height and length of each vehicle.
Ensure your load does not exceed the maximum allowable height for your location.
Height allowance: 13' 6" standard (though some states allow 14').
Height may be shorter in some markets. Always review your route and decrease the height based on road conditions.
3
Route Plan: Understand which vehicles will be unloaded first. Plan your load placement in the most efficient order. If possible, only move the vehicle twice (once during loading, once during unloading).
Non-Runner Rule: For vehicles that don't start, contact management to follow the No Runner Standard Work. Remember that a 2 Car hauler cannot move a no runner (more than a jump start required).

Typical Arrangements

Moving Two Vehicles:
First Vehicle: Small to midsize sedan or SUV, reversed in.
Second Vehicle: Midsize to large sedan or SUV, driven in forward.
Moving One Vehicle:
Single Vehicle: Pull in forward as far as possible to balance axle weights.

Specialty Vehicles Guide

Specialty vehicles fall outside the normal size range (e.g. modified vehicles or unmodified larger vehicles). Exercise extra caution due to their unique clearances and step-in heights.

1
Extra Caution: Use extra caution when moving around and getting in/out of specialty vehicles, as clearances and step-in heights differ drastically from normal vehicles.
2
Loading Tall, Heavy, or Wide Vehicles: If moving cargo vans or dually trucks, they will be the **only** vehicle loaded onto the truck. Ensure it is fully pulled forward onto the wheel stopper behind the cab.
3
Locating Tires: Locate the front tires as you would a normal pick-up truck and ensure the inside rear wheels are located as normal single rear tires of a pick-up truck would be.
4
Verifying Clearances & Weight:
Always verify height before transportation and know the clearances along your route.
Read clearance signs before crossing under bridges, underpasses, or other height obstructions.
Vehicle weights can be found in the Black Book Invoicing Guides (purchasing office or online at blackbookusa.com).
5
Determining Newer Vehicle Weights: Use the Gross Vehicle Weight Rating (GVWR) on the VIN tag and subtract the cargo and occupant weight found on the **Tire and Loading Information** tag on the driver's door jamb.
